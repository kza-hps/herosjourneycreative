# Glossary Pronunciation Source Notes

This note records the source basis and editorial method for the pronunciation
guide in `public/journal/Ho and the Baby Eater - Glossary Publication.docx`.

The glossary uses reader-friendly approximations, not IPA. They are intended to
help a general reader attempt the words aloud while preserving the real spelling
as the authority. The author should override any approximation that conflicts
with intended Kafiki usage, family/tribal usage, or fluent-speaker judgement.

## Approved Reference Sources

- University of Waikato, "Māori Pronunciation":
  https://www.waikato.ac.nz/students/maori/pronunciation/
  - Used for stress guidance: main stress usually lies on the first long vowel;
    where no long vowel exists, diphthongs matter; otherwise stress tends toward
    the earliest syllable within the relevant mora window.
  - Used for the treatment of vowels, consonant-vowel patterns, and diphthongs.

- Massey University, "Pronunciation - Whakahuatanga":
  https://www.massey.ac.nz/student-life/m%C4%81ori-massey/m%C4%81orimassey/ng%C4%81-rauemi-reo-m%C4%81ori-me-te-tikanga-m%C4%81ori/pronunciation-whakahuatanga/
  - Used for the short/long vowel distinction, macron guidance, and the reminder
    that adjacent different vowels keep their basic sound and run together.

- Te Aka Māori Dictionary, dictionary help and audio-entry policy:
  https://maoridictionary.co.nz/dictionary-help
  - Used for macron/tohutō handling and as an audio-first check point for known
    Māori headwords.

- Te Taura Whiri i te Reo Māori, "Guidelines for Māori Language Orthography":
  https://assets.nationbuilder.com/tetaurawhiri/pages/27659/attachments/original/1644789635/Guidelines_for_Maori_Language_Orthography.pdf
  - Used for orthography/source integrity: macrons are the established written
    means of marking long vowels in most Māori words and names.

- University of Hawaiʻi, "About Hawaiian diacritical marks":
  https://www.hawaii.edu/site/info/diacritics.php
  - Used for Hawaiian names in the glossary: ʻokina marks a glottal stop; kahakō
    lengthens the vowel and may add stress.

## Approximation Method

- The original spelling is authoritative. The bracketed guide is only a reader
  aid.
- Syllables are divided with hyphens.
- CAPS mark the approximated stressed syllable.
- Macroned vowels in the original word must be held longer, even where the
  English respelling cannot show length cleanly.
- `wh` is approximated as `f` for most standard Māori readings.
- `ng` is kept as `ng`, including word-initially; readers should use the sound
  in "singer," not the hard `g` in "finger."
- `r` should be a light tap/flap, not a heavy English `r`.
- For known Māori words, stress is guided by the Waikato rules above.
- For in-world names, mixed-Polynesian terms, English phrases, and story-specific
  forms, approximations are provisional and should be corrected by author review.
