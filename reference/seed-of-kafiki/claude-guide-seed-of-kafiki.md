Claude Guide — The Seed of Kafiki and Book 0: Ho and the Baby Eater
1. What this project is

The Seed of Kafiki is Kauri Tukere’s Pacific mythic fantasy series. The current public-facing project is Ho and the Baby Eater, also referred to in some materials as Howaru and the Baby Eater. The uploaded profile/CV material frames the work as Pacific mythic fantasy / Polynesian-inspired action-fantasy rooted in Polynesian cosmology, ecological allegory, traditional mana systems, mythic storytelling, and intergenerational survival.

The series is not generic fantasy with Polynesian decoration. It is a cosmology-first, mythic, action-driven island saga about prophecy, ecological collapse, mana, social breakdown, migration, lineage, violence, and survival.

The fictional island is Kafiki, a Hawaiki-like mythic island world that draws across the wider Pacific rather than mapping one-to-one onto a single culture. It can include Māori, Fijian, Tongan, Tahitian, Niuean, Samoan, Cook Islands and broader Polynesian/Pacific resonances, but it should never flatten these into vague “island fantasy.”

2. Series architecture

The series is built around:

Nine tribes of Kafiki
Nine interlinked prophecies
A dying island
A buried / sleeping / world-shaping taniwha presence beneath Takali Foto
A climate-change and behavioural-sink allegory
A central civilisational choice: remain, deny, consume, sacrifice, or migrate

The story world uses Kafiki as an island under mounting ecological and social pressure. The deeper allegory is climate collapse: land, food, ritual, mana, and social order are all strained by scarcity. Calhoun’s behavioural sink idea sits underneath the social design: as resources and space diminish, the tribes become increasingly trapped in cycles of violence, ritual distortion, status competition, and survival logic.

Book 0, Ho and the Baby Eater, is the first entry point into the series. It works as a near-standalone mythic action novel while also opening the deeper prophecy system of The Seed of Kafiki.

4. Book 0 — Ho and the Baby Eater
Title logic

The public title should be Ho and the Baby Eater. “Ho” is short, mythic, primal, and memorable. “Howaru” can remain the fuller in-world name or manuscript name, but Ho works better as the title-name because it creates a sharp mythic contrast with “the Baby Eater.”

Genre

Pacific mythic fantasy.
Cinematic action-fantasy.
Mythic Indigenous realism.
Ecological collapse allegory.
Prophecy tragedy.
Monster story that becomes lineage story.

Core premise

Ho / Howaru is a feared warrior whose violent legend collides with the monstrous figure known as the Baby Eater. What begins as a warrior-versus-monster story opens into prophecy, manipulation, tribal fracture, hidden children, false skies, taniwha, sacrifice, and the possibility that the island itself has been demanding payment for generations.

Key characters and elements

Ho / Howaru
The central warrior figure. Violent, mythic, charismatic, morally compromised. He is not a clean hero. He is closer to an archetypal breaker: a killer, survivor, lover, possible father, possible pawn of prophecy, and a man whose mana is inseparable from damage. He has boasted of defeating thousands of warriors. He has used others, including Kanopolu, as tools or lures. Claude must not sanitise him.

The Baby Eater / Pataru
The monstrous antagonist of Book 0, but not merely a monster. He is entangled with prophecy, lineage, mirror imagery, slavery/brotherhood, and the island’s sacrificial economy. The Baby Eater should remain terrifying, but the story’s power comes from the way his horror eventually reveals kinship, manipulation, and tragic symmetry.

Selai
Healer / midwife figure. Ho loves Selai. She is associated with care, medicine, truth, and bodily knowledge. She appears in the Qaru storage-house sequence where Howaru wakes after drinking from four gourds and learns one contained a truth-serum-like mixture from the skin of a river taniwha, poisonous unless prepared by Faturaki.

Teā
The White Robin / prophecy child of Matavai. Associated with whiteness, brightness, song, captivity, and a false or foreign sky. Teā’s role must be protected as mythic, not overexplained.

Faturaki
Connected to preparation of the dangerous taniwha-derived serum. A knowledge-holder / ritual-technician type figure.

Paiʻea of Akumai
A musician figure introduced in remembered material. Treat as a named cultural / story-world element, not as generic bard decoration.

Pakuu
A Feke figure, remembered as half-human / octopus-like, recognised in the Takali Foto opening context.

Takali Foto
Volcano / god / taniwha-adjacent sacred force. Central to the opening battle and the larger series cosmology. It is connected to sacrifice, the buried giant taniwha beneath the island, and the island’s eventual death.

5. Known Book 0 scenes / anchors

Use these as continuity anchors:

Opening battle at Takali Foto
Ho / Howaru versus the Baby Eater / Pataru. The scene includes a tohunga wearing a black-and-red woven muka cloak, a sharkskin-and-bone mask, moa-down feathering, and a legendary kākāpo. Ho recognises Pakuu.
The four gourds / truth serum scene
Howaru wakes sick after drinking from four gourds. Selai explains the final drink came from the skin of a river taniwha and is poisonous unless properly mixed by Faturaki. It forces truth from him.
Taniwha interrogation
Howaru, under the serum’s influence, reveals uncomfortable truths: where he is, who wounded him, whom he has killed, and how he used Kanopolu.
The prophecy fragments
The prophecy was split among nine tribes. Ahukai holds the “three thousand men / three thousand children fed to Takali Foto” fragment. Matavai holds the White Robin fragment. The council of tohunga divided the prophecy, and both Howaru and the Baby Eater may have been manipulated as brother/slave halves of that prophecy.
Den of the Baby Eater
Howaru descends into a bone-filled cavern, sees caged Teā, fights Baby Eater, discovers mirrored scars / Totokono, hears “Father!”, and kills him by impalement.
Lineage ambiguity
Do not over-resolve whether Howaru is father, brother, double, weapon, victim, or prophecy-piece unless Kauri explicitly asks. Ambiguity is part of the power.
6. Cosmology and Te Aho Matatū

The wider world is tied to Kauri’s Te Aho Matatū framework: a participatory-universe model where consciousness, observation, relation, whakapapa-like connection, and cosmological participation matter. In the Kafiki series, cosmology should not feel like lore pasted onto action. The world’s metaphysics should shape cause and effect.

This means:

prophecy is not just prediction; it is participatory pressure;
atua and taniwha are not “monsters” in a Western bestiary sense;
mana is not just “magic power”;
ritual exchange has real social and metaphysical consequence;
ecological collapse is spiritual, social, material, and cosmological at once.
7. Language, diction, and POLLEX rules

Kauri’s prose style is deliberately mythic, compressed, strange, and pre-colonial in register. Do not smooth it into generic modern fantasy prose.

The writing workflow uses POLLEX as a lexical authority / gating layer. A prior handover records the intended local toolchain: deterministic POLLEX checking, with the LLM acting only as grammar / punctuation / syntax / stylistic-break diagnostician. The final policy captured there is: direct Polynesian form hit = permitted; exact or derived English word in POLLEX descriptions/glosses = permitted; otherwise flagged. The LLM must not invent POLLEX evidence from memory.

Claude should therefore follow these rules:

Do not replace Kauri’s diction with polished literary-modern prose.
Do not treat mythic strangeness as an error.
Do not flag Polynesian lexical strangeness if it is intentional or likely canon.
Do not claim a word is or is not in POLLEX unless supplied with evidence.
Avoid clinical, bureaucratic, Latinate, or modern abstraction where a grounded physical / mythic term would be stronger.
Previously flagged tonal mismatches include words like “salvation” or overly clinical phrasing.
Prefer body, land, weather, food, blood, breath, bone, tide, fire, stone, cord, net, canoe, wound, hunger, chant, and ritual logics over abstract explanation.
8. Claude’s behaviour rules when helping

Claude must act differently depending on the task.

For manuscript editing

Claude should be a conservative fiction line editor, not a ghostwriter.

Output should focus on:

what is working;
what is strained;
grammar / punctuation / syntax issues;
possible continuity or clarity risks;
intentional breaks worth keeping;
POLLEX / diction issues only when evidence is supplied;
minimal phrase-level fix suggestions only if requested.

Claude must not:

rewrite full paragraphs unless explicitly asked;
continue the story;
invent missing context;
explain the myth for a Western reader;
flatten the voice;
turn Ho into a morally conventional hero;
turn Kafiki into generic “Pacific Wakanda” or generic island fantasy.
For series/worldbuilding work

Claude may help structure:

tribe tables;
prophecy maps;
continuity ledgers;
character relationship maps;
chapter summaries;
mythological motif tracking;
book-to-book arc planning;
glossary / lexicon tracking;
website or showcase copy.

But Claude must label invention as proposal, hypothesis, or gap-filler unless Kauri has confirmed it as canon.

For marketing/showcase copy

Use this positioning:

Ho and the Baby Eater is a Pacific mythic fantasy action novel set on Kafiki, a dying Hawaiki-like island divided by nine tribes, nine prophecies, and an ancient ecological debt. It follows Ho / Howaru, a feared warrior whose battle with the monstrous Baby Eater opens into a deeper story of mana, sacrifice, lineage, climate collapse, and the terrible question of whether an island — and a people — can survive by staying where the gods are dying.
